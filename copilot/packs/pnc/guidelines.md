# Commercial Package Underwriting Guidelines (Synthetic)

These guidelines are fictional and exist only to demonstrate the copilot. They are
written the way a real carrier's appetite guide reads so retrieval is realistic.

## 1 Purpose and authority

This guide sets appetite for small and middle-market commercial property and
general liability accounts. Underwriters may bind accounts that meet every
guideline. Accounts that trip a referral trigger must be reviewed by a senior
underwriter before quoting. Declined classes may not be quoted.

## 2 Eligible business classes

### 2.1 Target classes

Preferred classes include fabricated metal manufacturing, hardware wholesalers,
home centers, engineering services, full-service restaurants, plumbing and HVAC
contractors, lessors of nonresidential buildings and retail bakeries.

### 2.2 Referral classes

Chemical manufacturing, solid waste collection and aircraft manufacturing carry
elevated severity potential. These NAICS classes require senior underwriter
referral with a completed supplemental hazard questionnaire.

### 2.3 Prohibited classes

Explosives manufacturing and crude petroleum extraction are prohibited classes.
Do not quote; decline the submission and notify the broker.

### 2.4 Unlisted classes

A NAICS code that is not listed in this guide is outside documented appetite and
must be referred for a class determination before quoting.

## 3 Property

### 3.1 Total insured value limits

Maximum total insured value (TIV) per account is $25,000,000. Accounts with TIV
above $15,000,000 require referral for capacity and reinsurance review. Accounts
above $25,000,000 must be declined.

### 3.2 Fire protection class

Locations in ISO public protection class 9 or 10 have limited fire department
response. Decline unless every building is fully sprinklered; sprinklered
risks in protection class 9 or 10 are referred.

### 3.3 Building age and construction

Buildings constructed before 1960 must be referred with evidence of updated
roof, electrical, plumbing and heating systems.

## 4 Loss history

### 4.1 Loss ratio thresholds

The five-year loss ratio (incurred losses divided by earned premium) is the
primary loss history test. Above 65% requires referral. Above 100% must be
declined unless a senior underwriter documents a corrective action plan.

### 4.2 Large or severe single losses

Any single claim with incurred value of $250,000 or more must be referred,
regardless of the overall loss ratio, with a description of the cause and
the corrective measures taken.

### 4.3 Loss run requirements

Currently valued carrier loss runs covering at least three years are required.
Five years are preferred. Submissions with missing loss runs or fewer than three
years of history must be referred.

## 5 Account characteristics

### 5.1 Years in business

Businesses operating fewer than three years lack a reliable track record and
must be referred.

### 5.2 Licensed states

The program writes in TX, OK, LA, AR, NM, KS, MO and CO. Risks domiciled in
other states must be declined because we are not licensed there.

## 6 Submission completeness

### 6.1 Required information

The applicant name, state, NAICS code, years in business, annual revenue,
statement of values and loss runs are required. Incomplete submissions must be
referred and returned to the broker for the missing information.

## 7 Analytics

### 7.1 Modeled risk referral

The large-loss propensity model is an advisory tool. When the modeled
probability of a large loss in the next policy year is 25% or higher, refer the
account for senior review even if every other guideline is met. The model never
declines an account on its own.
